# Synthetic data: assumptions and rationale

**No real financial data was used in this project.** No real AML transaction
data is public, for reasons that do not need explaining. Everything here is
generated by `src/generate_data.py` from the seed in `config/params.yml`.

The alternative to synthetic data was not real data; it was no project. What
follows is every assumption that materially affects a reported number, so the
analysis can be argued with rather than taken on trust.

## Provenance of the typologies

The five illicit patterns are modelled on money laundering methods described in
FINTRAC's published operational alerts and typology guidance, and in the broader
public literature on the subject: structuring below the Large Cash Transaction
Report threshold, funnel accounts, rapid movement of funds, third-party cash
deposits inconsistent with a declared business, and layering through round-value
transfers.

What is modelled is the **behavioural signature** — the shape of the activity in
a transaction ledger. No real case data was used, and no real typology document
is reproduced.

## Population

| Parameter | Value | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Customers | 4,000 | Large enough for stable rates, small enough to run in CI |
| Period | 180 days | Supports a 90-day feature window plus a 90-day evaluation period |
| Business share | 22% | Rough shape of a commercial-and-retail book |
| Illicit customer rate | 1.2% | Deliberately conservative. Estimates of laundered funds run 2-5% of global GDP; the share of *customers* involved is far lower and genuinely unknown |
| Hard negatives | 138 (3.5%) | Lawful customers with typology-shaped behaviour |
| Transactions | ~469,000 | Emergent from the above |

## The three-layer construction

**Layer 1 — baseline.** Ordinary activity for every customer, with volume
anchored to declared income, weekday bias, and channel mix varying by segment
and cash intensity.

**Layer 2 — legitimate but alert-triggering.** Applied to *every* customer,
including illicit ones. Cash businesses banking daily takings, remittance
corridors, property completions, recurring round-value rent and loan payments,
seasonal revenue spikes, secondary accounts waking up. This layer is the reason
the false positive rate is realistic; without it the rules were 89% precise and
there was nothing to triage.

**Layer 3 — illicit.** Five typologies overlaid on top of a customer's ordinary
activity, never replacing it, in a bursty campaign window of ~30 days.

**Hard negatives.** 138 lawful customers given typology-shaped behaviour. Added
after the second modelling round showed the two populations were nearly
linearly separable — see `WHAT_DIDNT_WORK.md`.

## Injected data defects

Deliberate, and each one has a corresponding control in `04_data_quality.sql`.

| Defect | Rate | Represents |
|---|---|---|
| Duplicate rows | ~0.4% | A replayed source feed |
| Missing counterparty country on wires | ~3% | Incomplete SWIFT field capture |
| Country code casing and whitespace | ~2% | A legacy system with no input validation |
| Orphan account identifiers | 40 rows | Account master out of sync with transaction feed |
| Negative amounts | ~0.1% | Mis-signed reversals |

## What is realistic, and what is not

**Reasonably realistic**

- Alert volume: ~132 per day for a 4,000-customer book
- Rule precision by rule: 2.5% to 22%
- Overall false positive rate: **94.7%**, inside the 90-98% band commonly cited
- Full detection coverage: all 48 illicit customers generate at least one alert
- The dominant false positive sources are cash-intensive business and lawful
  corridor activity, which matches practitioner accounts

**Not realistic, and material**

- **Labels are perfect.** Ground truth is known by construction. Real labels are
  investigator dispositions: noisy, inconsistent between analysts, and observed
  only for alerts someone actually worked.
- **Typologies are static.** Real launderers adapt against detection. Nothing
  here responds to the rules.
- **Populations are cleaner than reality.** Even with hard negatives, the
  overlap is almost certainly less severe than a real book.
- **No customer network structure.** Counterparties are random identifiers with
  no persistent relationships, which removes the entire network-analysis
  dimension where real programmes find organised activity.
- **Every illicit customer is detectable.** Real monitoring misses typologies it
  was not written for, and there is no way to measure that from inside the
  system.

## The honest bottom line

Reported performance is an **upper bound on a simplified problem**. The
transferable content of this project is the pipeline, the controls, the
evaluation discipline and the operating policy — not the numbers. Any claim that
this model would defer 15.9% of a real bank's alert volume would be unfounded,
and I would not make it in an interview.
